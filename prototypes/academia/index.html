150 years of students measuring the same river

In 1876, Halbrook's first chemistry class walked to the Wend with glass bottles and a notebook. Every autumn since, first-year students have gone back to the same bend to sample the water. The record is now one of the longest river surveys in Europe.

Read the story
1876
The first class takes twelve samples and records them by hand.
1931
The survey becomes part of every first-year science course.
1974
Students install the first automatic gauge on the riverbank.
2026
Over 4,800 students have now added to the same record.

All research news

  • City parks store more carbon than the farmland around them

    Undergraduates sampled 300 sites across Halbrook and found the oldest parks hold almost twice the carbon of nearby fields.

    Soil ecology · Global Change Biology, October 2026

  • A shipping ledger from 1910 explains a missing generation

    A misfiled ledger in the university archive shows where 2,000 families from the port went when the records stop.

    History · Journal of Social History, September 2026

  • Older bees teach younger ones where the flowers are

    Tracking 900 bumblebees showed that new foragers copy the routes of experienced ones for their first three days.

    Behavioural ecology · Current Biology, September 2026
